# Expansion 01 — source-located Classical Nahuatl vocabulary

**Edition target:** 2026-10-27.4.0. **Scope:** 15 *new* headwords;
all 88 existing entries and their 674 existing field claims remain unchanged.

The authoritative source is the [Online Nahuatl Dictionary](https://nahuatl.wired-humanities.org/),
edited by Stephanie Wood, especially its **specific, visible transcriptions**
of Molina (1571), Karttunen (1992), Lockhart (2001), and named colonial
editions. The individual URL and locator are stored **per field claim**, not
just in a general bibliography. The pages were examined against their printed
transcription text; original folio images were **not independently collated**
for this batch. Accordingly, “source-located” is stronger than “generated”
but is **not** a claim of independent manuscript verification.

## Inclusion and evidence rules

1. A source-located **headword, core gloss and grammatical category** are
   required before an entry is published. Each comes with a named exact
   dictionary entry and, where present, printed folio or page.
2. A **historically quoted token** is graded `text` only with a contextual
   original-language quote and dated edition locator. A form in Molina,
   Karttunen, or Lockhart's *paradigm* is graded `dictionary` or `grammar`,
   not an independently attested manuscript occurrence.
3. **Stem segmentation** is upgraded only where the lexicon supplies an
   explicit segmenting notation or discussed combining form. Unclear
   allomorphy is left open.
4. **General nominal animacy** remains `inference`, except where a particular
   grammar actually diagnoses the lexeme. Inanimate English translation is
   not a full Nahuatl agreement demonstration.
5. Empty **plural** and **possessed** fields mean *no fully checked
   lexeme-specific token in this pass*, not that such forms cannot occur.
6. The 12 prior 10×6 paradigms remain unchanged. Irregular newly published
   verb tokens **increase the analyzer's cited-form coverage** without
   pretending that a complete conjugational matrix was verified.
7. Distinguish `tlacuilo` the occupational noun, `cuiloa` the transitive
   verb, and `tlacuiloa` the lexicalized intransitive writing verb.

## Remaining scholarly limits

These are **edition-verified lexical citations through the ODN's published
transcriptions**, not fresh independent paleographic verification. The
original manuscripts can have different spellings or vocalic quantity.
No automatic contextual syntactic disambiguation is claimed. Particularly,
`tlacuiloque` has more than one reading, `yauh` is suppletive, and
`āyōtl`'s dictionary plural suffix is **not** a fully located plural token.

Run `tools/test_expansion01.py`, existing Phase 1–4 tests and the browser
morphology tests before publication. The newest source-located forms must
remain `dictionary` / `text` as their citations warrant, not generated
historical “certainties.”
