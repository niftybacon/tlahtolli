# Historical spellings

Each dictionary word displays its recorded spelling(s) at the bottom of its Overview. The existing `original` field is an **editorial spelling record**, not proof of independent manuscript attestation. Source-located `references.json` witnesses are labeled with the source collection, exact historical form, dated work or period, precise folio/section and link.

## Source evidence

- The Getty Digital Florentine Codex [Book 1, folio 16r](https://florentinecodex.getty.edu/en/book/1/folio/16r), Anderson–Dibble Nahuatl transcription, records `Jn` in the phrase “Jn aqujn…” and `yn` in a different clause. Both are spellings of the particle `in`.
- The capital `J` is preserved exactly as in the transcription; it is not automatically substituted for `i` throughout the dictionary. Historic orthography was not standardized.
- An entry-recorded spelling is separated from a manuscript witness. A manuscript occurrence does not independently establish animacy, pluralization, word sense, or a complete inflectional paradigm.
- The linked witnesses span different time periods and regions; their sources must not be conflated.
- Transcription material may be copyrighted: show only a short identifying form and a link rather than reproducing extensive passages.

No changes are made to lexical headwords, morphology tables, personal browser data, study schedules, or user overrides. The offline source atlas is refreshed with the same evidence.
