# Animacy and number: historical grammatical guide

**Evidence grades are not interchangeable.** “Dictionary” denotes a lexicographically recorded form; “text” a dated written occurrence; “grammar” an explicit published rule/example; “inference” a proposed application to a particular word **without** independent lexeme-specific evidence; “unverified” means evidence still missing. None implies specialist peer review.

Michel Launey's *An Introduction to Classical Nahuatl* (Cambridge, 2011), §§1.5 and 2.2–2.6 (pp.15–21) establishes that nominal citation forms can serve with singular or plural referents. Inanimate **calli** and **tetl**, unlike ordinary English 'house(s)' and 'stone(s)', may remain formally unchanged even with plural reference. **Ātl** is a mass noun, but unchanged reference applies beyond English mass nouns. Animate beings usually show distinct lexical plurals; the pattern cannot be predicted from singular spelling alone.

**Tēpetl** (mountain) and **cītlalin** (star) are classic pluralizable exceptions, possibly through personification; this is not a universal rule that all nonliving things with plural forms are now grammatically animate. Other plurals may be late colonial or used in specialized figurative senses. A nominal plural is not evidence for each individual word sense, location or period. The lack of an attested plural in our database means **not documented**, not **impossible**.

Regular stem stripping is recorded as an editorial **inference** rather than as an independently manuscript-attested form. All predicted stem analyses retain their grammar reference, locator and warning.

Do not auto-promote individual inference records merely because a general grammar rule exists.
