## Corrected misleading displays

- `tecpan` was generically marked `none (agentive/locative)`; it is now explicitly a locative/relational **and** nominal item, not an agentive (Lockhart 2001, p.233).
- `tlatoani`, `tlamatini` and `teopixqui` no longer receive the blanket 'agentive/locative' label; their source-specific agentive analyses are shown.
- The `āltepētl` possessive has two separately prefixed components; it is not a single suffix-exchange rule.
- `itzcuintli` displays only historically quoted plural variants, with a separately qualified **possessed plural** witness.
- `icxitl` remains **without a generalized absolute plural**, despite quoted possessed-body-part and dialectal/Spanish-influenced forms (Launey 2011 p.97, Townsend 2010 p.51).
- `tlamantli` remains without a general free-noun plural: Launey 2011 p.64's `ōntlamantin` is a **numbered category/counter** example, not interchangeable with 'things'.

## Evidence rules / still outstanding

**Grammar** is an explicit published example or lexeme-specific rule. **Dictionary** is a lexicographically documented form or stem. **Text** denotes a contextual historical wording supplied in an identified edition of a colonial record; some have not been rechecked against folio images. **Inference** remains a candidate lexical analysis derived from rules (62 initial stems and 51 initial animacy hypotheses). **Unverified** means no adequate lexeme-specific cited field has yet been established in this review, not that the proposed form is unattested anywhere.

Do **not** infer complete plural or possessive paradigms from a single witness, combine regional forms silently, claim that semantic inanimacy proves agreement class, or upgrade suffix-only dictionary notation into a full-word manuscript attestation. The per-claim CSV sets the next targeted research question for each remaining field.
